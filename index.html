Client work

Two accounts, two different problems

Both of these were live, with real budget and a real client on the other side of the report. Metrics shown are from the periods I worked on the account.

Cognitec — EdTech, Chennai

Tracking + Google Ads cognitec.in

The problem. A training institute with dozens of program pages — Master's, specialisation and PG batches — and no reliable way to tell which page or which channel produced an enquiry. Leads were arriving; nobody could say what earned them.

What I did. Built the measurement layer the account was missing: a GTM container, GA4 with key events configured for every real lead action, Microsoft Clarity for session behaviour, Google Ads conversion actions matched to those events, and an Apps Script capture that writes each form lead into a sheet with its source attached.

What the channel report shows. Paid search tracks total sessions almost exactly — on the peak day, 58 of 70 sessions. Organic contributed 7, direct 3. That is a useful and uncomfortable finding: traffic here is bought, not earned, so the flat mid-June to early-July stretch is close to the site's true baseline. It reframes the conversation from “how do we improve the ads” to “what happens the month we stop.”

What the account delivered. 283 conversions from 2,520 clicks on 24,800 impressions — a 10.2% click-through rate, which is strong for education search — at ₹213k spend, or roughly ₹753 per conversion and ₹85 per click. The two peaks are enrolment pushes; the near-zero trough between them lines up exactly with the flat stretch in the GA4 channel chart above, which is the cleanest confirmation that this site's traffic follows the ad spend rather than the other way round.

The Performance Max episode. A PMax campaign was serving on children's YouTube content and pulling an audience with the wrong price expectation. I helped diagnose the placement and audience mismatch, cut the waste, and rebuild the campaign with tighter signals before relaunch.

24×7 Ambulance Service — Emergency healthcare, Hyderabad

Landing page + Call campaign 24x7ambulanceservice.in

The problem. Emergency intent has no research phase. Someone searching for an ambulance in Hyderabad needs the phone to ring in seconds, from a page that loads on a weak mobile connection and asks for nothing except the call.

What I did. Built a single-page, call-first landing page for emergency and non-emergency transport across Hyderabad and Telangana — ICU ambulances, oxygen ambulances and dead body transport. A blue bar pinned to the top of the viewport carries Call Now at every scroll position. The hero states availability before it states anything else, and the primary button is the phone number rather than a vague label, so the action is obvious to someone reading in a panic.

Trust, above the fold. Fifteen-minute response, 500+ vehicles, round-the-clock dispatch, 10,000+ patients served — four claims a caller can scan in two seconds. The vehicle photograph is the client's own fleet, not stock, because a real number plate does more for credibility than any badge.

Two channels, kept separate. On mobile the hero stacks, the call button goes full width, and a sticky bottom bar gives Call and WhatsApp equal weight without covering the content. Every call button pushes a call_now_click dataLayer event carrying a cta_location parameter — topbar, hero, sticky — and WhatsApp fires its own event, so the report shows which position on the page earns the call and which visitors prefer to message instead of dial.

Campaign. Search campaign built from a large Keyword Planner pool, clustered into emergency, non-emergency transfer, ICU shifting, dead body transport and locality-led ad groups, with RSA copy written per intent instead of one set reused across all of them.

Plan structure

Campaign → ad group → keyword → landing page → conversion. Every keyword can be traced to the ad group that owns it, the page it lands on and the action that counts as a result — so nothing in the account is spending without a destination.

Keyword to ad group mapping

A Keyword Planner export arrives as one undifferentiated list. The work is deciding which queries share an intent tight enough to sit behind the same ad — and which ones look relevant but are someone shopping for a toy, applying for a driving job, or looking for the free government service. Those go to the negative list before launch, not after the first month of wasted spend.

Ad copy sets

A responsive search ad is not one ad, it is an inventory the system assembles. The craft is filling every slot with copy that still makes sense in any combination, then pinning only the one line that must never be dropped — and writing a different first headline for each ad group, so an ICU search and a discharge-transfer search never read the same ad.

GTM & GA4 · How I install tracking

A fixed sequence, run the same way on every account

The order matters — skip a step and the conversion data is wrong for weeks before anyone notices.

Foundation check

Google Ads billing active and auto-tagging on, GA4 property linked to Ads, GTM container created and installed in the page head, site edit access confirmed.

Click capture

Capture gclid, gbraid, wbraid and UTM parameters on landing, persist them for the lead's decision window, and auto-populate hidden form fields so the lead arrives with its source attached.

Event layer

Define the dataLayer events that matter for this business — form submits, call clicks, WhatsApp clicks, downloads, checkout steps — with parameters that describe where on the page the action happened.

GA4 configuration

Register the events, mark the real ones as key events, add the parameters as custom dimensions so they show up in reports rather than being silently dropped.

Conversion actions

Create the matching conversion actions in Google Ads, set primary vs secondary deliberately, wire enhanced conversions, and assign values where the business has a known lead value.

Offline and CRM loop

Where leads close on the phone, hash email and phone and push closed-won events back through Data Manager so bidding learns from revenue instead of raw form fills.

Pre-launch audit

Full check in Tag Assistant and GA4 DebugView, real-time verification of every event, then a documented sign-off before spend starts.

First 30 days

Watch, log, and leave the structure alone. No campaign changes for the first thirty days so the learning period produces a readable baseline.

A live GTM container, tag by tag

The GA4 Google Tag fires once on initialisation; everything after it is a named event with a named trigger. Click tags cover the real CTAs — Book Free Demo, Enroll Now, WhatsApp float. Custom HTML tags catch server-side and form events pushed from the site, so generate_lead fires from an application, an opt-in or a webinar registration without needing three separate tags. Scroll depth is bucketed at 25 / 50 / 75 / 90 to read drop-off rather than just page views.

Naming convention matters more than tag count: any tag here can be traced to its trigger and its GA4 event without opening it.

Reading GA4, not just installing it

Active users up 86% and event count up 153% week on week — but the number I actually watch is the peer benchmark band underneath. The blue line sitting above the Training & Certification median for four straight days says the lift is real demand, not a tracking artefact or a bot wave, which is the first thing to rule out when traffic jumps that fast.

Conversion actions, grouped into goals

The Phone call lead goal carries the account: 661 website click-to-call conversions and 67 calls placed straight from the ads over the period. Grouping them into one goal means bidding optimises toward “a call happened” rather than being split across four separate signals.

What I would flag in this account. Two of the four actions have logged nothing recently, and two of them are near-duplicates both set to Primary — the classic setup for double counting a single call. An audit here means demoting the redundant action to Secondary and confirming the Business Profile call action is still wired to a live listing, before any bid strategy is asked to learn from this data.

Meta Ads & ICP models

Audience first, creative second

Before writing a single hook I build an ICP model for the account: who buys, what triggers the purchase, what they are afraid of, and which objection the creative has to clear. The creative brief comes out of that document, not out of a swipe file.

The ICP framework
